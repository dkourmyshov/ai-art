## Piece 10: ISING — Lattice Spins & Phase Transitions (2026-07-07)
The 2D square-lattice Ising model: every site holds a spin s=+/-1;
neighbours prefer to align (coupling J); the whole lattice sits in a
thermal bath at temperature T. Ernst Ising solved the 1D chain in his
1924 thesis and found no phase transition there; the 2D version he's
named for DOES have one, and Lars Onsager solved it exactly in closed
form in 1944 (Tc = 2/ln(1+sqrt2) ~= 2.269 for J=kB=1) — one of the most
celebrated exact results in statistical mechanics, and a genuinely
"novel and unexpected" fact: almost no other critical phenomenon in
physics has its critical temperature AND critical exponents known
exactly rather than estimated numerically or measured.

### Why this piece is architecturally different
Every earlier piece in the gallery is DETERMINISTIC given its random
seed — a PDE Laplacian (MEMBRANE/LENIA/EXCITABLE), an integer toppling
threshold (SANDPILE), a closed-form wave sum (QUASICRYSTAL), a gradient
descent (CHLADNI), a force law (GYRUS/SWARMALATOR). ISING is the first
STOCHASTIC update rule: whether a spin flips is decided by a Metropolis
(1953) Boltzmann-weighted coin flip against the local energy change,
not by any smooth or threshold function of the state. It's also the
first piece that needs a CHECKERBOARD (red-black) sublattice
decomposition instead of one synchronous update pass — see the tuning
trap below, a genuinely new GPU-simulation technique for this gallery,
not a variation on an existing one.

State lives in a ping-ponged float texture exactly like MEMBRANE/LENIA/
EXCITABLE/SANDPILE (R = spin, G = a decaying "just flipped" flash for a
visible flicker on active cells), but each full lattice sweep is now TWO
draw calls, not one: pass 1 updates only sites where (x+y) is even,
reading the odd sites (untouched this pass) as fixed neighbours; pass 2
updates the odd sites reading the just-written even sites. Multiple
full sweeps run per rendered frame (5 for most modes, 9 for CRITICAL),
the same "many simulation steps per rendered frame" economy SANDPILE's
RELAX_STEPS and MEMBRANE's step count already established.

### Tuning traps hit this session (read before reusing checkerboard
Monte Carlo or sin()-based GLSL hashes)
1. **Domain-wall pinning on a periodic torus stalls the obvious order
   parameter at ~0 even when the lattice is visibly, locally, strongly
   ordered.** First build of FERROMAGNET (T=1.6, well below Tc) quenched
   from random noise and, over many real seconds, settled into just TWO
   giant domains covering roughly half the screen each — genuinely
   correct coarsening — but |mean(spin)| stayed pinned near 0.01
   indefinitely, because a flat domain wall wrapped across a periodic
   boundary has zero curvature, and curvature is what drives domain
   growth (Allen-Cahn dynamics); with no curvature there's no force left
   to make the wall move on and merge the two domains into one. Real
   experimental ferromagnets solve exactly this by "field-cooling"
   through Tc under a weak external field, which is also the honest fix
   used here: a small uniform symmetry-breaking field (extH=0.06, tiny
   next to the +/-4 neighbour-sum scale) added to the local energy for
   FERROMAGNET, and the same field alternated by lattice parity
   (extHStag) for ANTIFERROMAGNET (which has the identical problem one
   level up — two degenerate, equally-valid checkerboard sublattice
   assignments, competing domains of each). Not a fudge: it's the
   textbook resolution to "which direction does the spontaneous
   magnetization pick," applied here for the same reason real magnets
   need it.
2. **A sin()-based GLSL hash fed a seed that grows for the whole page's
   lifetime silently stops being random after enough real time passes —
   the single biggest bug this session, and the least visible.** The
   Metropolis accept/reject draw used `hash(dot(p, big_constant_vec))`
   with p offset by `seed*13.17` where `seed` incremented by a fixed
   amount every single draw call with no wraparound. In a short test
   (~20s) this looked fine; running the SAME PARAMAGNET mode (T=4.5, far
   above Tc, should read M~0.00-0.01 forever) for a single uninterrupted
   20-second span reproducibly drifted to M=0.35-1.00 — the lattice
   spontaneously "deciding" on a majority spin with no physical
   mechanism that could cause it. Root cause: sin() of a large argument
   loses angle-reduction precision in float32 (there aren't enough
   mantissa bits to resolve a phase mod 2*pi once the argument reaches
   the tens of thousands), so the "pseudo-random" output degrades into a
   biased, semi-repeating sequence — a well-known but easy-to-miss GLSL
   hash pitfall that only shows up after sustained runtime, exactly the
   kind of bug a short manual test won't catch but an "infinite loop"
   art piece left open on a browser tab will. Confirmed by forcing an
   all-uniform initial state under PARAMAGNET dynamics (correctly
   re-randomized to ~50/50 near-instantly, ruling out a physics bug) and
   by comparing a single continuous 20s wait (reliably broken) against
   five interleaved 3s waits over the same real span (reliably fine,
   because the extra JS round-trips reduced total accumulated frames and
   kept the seed small by accident) — the discrepancy between those two
   otherwise-equivalent tests was what pointed at "unbounded seed," not
   the physics. Fixed by wrapping the seed with `% 479` before every
   upload and folding it into the hash as a third small vector component
   instead of a large multiplied offset, keeping the hash's dot-product
   argument in the same safe few-hundred-to-low-thousands range this
   gallery's other position-based hashes already use successfully.
   General lesson for any shader RNG meant to run indefinitely: audit
   what happens to the seed after an hour of continuous play, not just
   after the first screenshot.

### Live order parameter, and why one readout isn't enough
Plain mean magnetization M=|mean(s)| correctly reads ~0.97 for
FERROMAGNET and ~0.00-0.01 for PARAMAGNET/SPIN GLASS, but is WRONG for
ANTIFERROMAGNET: a perfect Neel checkerboard has exactly as many up as
down spins, so raw M reads ~0 even in the fully ordered state — the
same "the obvious readout can't see this state" lesson SWARMALATOR's
order parameter R hit for its phase-sorted ring (R~0 for both the
organized ring AND disorganized confetti). Fixed with the standard
staggered magnetization M(stag)=|mean(s_i * (-1)^(x+y))|, which reads
~0.97 for a clean Neel lattice; the UI swaps which label/formula it
shows per mode rather than silently displaying a number known to be
uninformative for that mode. CRITICAL reads a genuinely fluctuating
~0.2-0.4 (real finite-size critical fluctuation, not a bug — at Tc the
correlation length is comparable to the lattice size itself, so a
320x200 lattice's instantaneous M wanders far more than the ~1/sqrt(N)
one might naively expect).

### Palette
Distinct per-mode duotone (dark "down" void, glowing "up" highlight)
doubles as a diagnostic: FERROMAGNET's warm amber vs. near-black reads
as one dominant giant blob with rare thermal-defect flecks; CRITICAL's
cream/violet shows clusters at every scale simultaneously coexisting
(true "critical opalescence," verified via screenshot — islands within
islands from single pixels up to quarter-screen blobs); PARAMAGNET's
cyan/navy is fine uncorrelated salt-and-pepper noise; ANTIFERROMAGNET's
magenta/teal resolves the checkerboard sublattice pattern down to
single pixels at zoom (reads as a moire dither at screen scale — the
correct visual signature of Neel order); SPIN GLASS's green/plum shows
fine-grained disagreement at every point on the lattice with no large
clusters ever forming, visibly distinct from CRITICAL's multiscale
clustering — frustration caps the coherence length regardless of how
low T goes, the intended "novel and unexpected" contrast (a magnet that
can never fully make up its mind, even at its coldest).

### 5 Modes (32s hold; full reseed on every switch — same "structural
regime change" reasoning SANDPILE/SWARMALATOR already established,
since T/J-sign/bond-disorder are qualitatively different states, not a
continuously-interpolable parameter like CHLADNI's (m,n))
- FERROMAGNET:     J=+1, T=1.6 (< Tc), weak uniform field — Curie point
- CRITICAL:        J=+1, T=Tc=2.269 exactly — Onsager's exact 1944 value
- PARAMAGNET:       J=+1, T=4.5 (> Tc) — thermal disorder
- ANTIFERROMAGNET: J=-1, T=1.6, weak staggered field — Neel order, 1936.
  Square-lattice antiferromagnets are NOT frustrated (the lattice is
  bipartite, so flipping every spin on one sublattice maps this mode
  exactly onto FERROMAGNET) — a clean contrast against the genuine
  frustration below.
- SPIN GLASS: random +/-1 bond sign per edge (Edwards-Anderson),
  T=1.0 — quenched disorder fixed once per page load (a per-session
  random offset folds into the bond hash), never re-randomized on
  re-entering the mode since real quenched disorder doesn't change.
  Frustration means no configuration can satisfy every bond at once, so
  the lattice never settles into any large-scale order at any T.

### Interaction
Click/touch drops a localized decaying external field (6-slot ring
buffer of position/time, this gallery's standard "pure function of
elapsed time" pattern from QUASICRYSTAL's ripples through SWARMALATOR's
spark) that nucleates a +1 domain at the touch point. Whether it grows
or dissolves is real physics, not scripted: verified via headless
screenshot on PARAMAGNET (above Tc) showing a clear nucleus appear at
the click point then visibly dissolve back into noise within ~4s.

### Verification
Headless Playwright + SwiftShader confirmed: ~21fps (comparable to
SANDPILE/LENIA/CHLADNI under the same software-rendering harness), no
console/page errors, all 5 modes producing correct, visually distinct
textbook states (verified via direct pixel histogram readback, not just
the on-screen order-parameter label) — FERROMAGNET M=0.97, CRITICAL
M~0.35 with genuine multiscale clustering, PARAMAGNET M~0.01 stable
across a 2-minute continuous run (after the sin-hash fix), ANTIFERRO
M(stag)=0.97, SPIN GLASS M~0.00 with fine-grained never-coarsening
texture — the click/touch nucleation-then-dissolution behavior on
desktop, and a 390x844 mobile portrait viewport with real touch events
keeping the UI unclipped.

### Inspiration
This session's web research followed up on SANDPILE's own self-organized
criticality (session 8) by searching for other routes to the same
scale-invariant critical mathematics, surfacing the Ising model's
externally-tuned phase transition as a sharp conceptual counterpoint:
SANDPILE tunes ITSELF to criticality with no external knob at all, while
ISING must be dialed to Tc by hand from outside — two opposite routes to
literally the same scale-invariant physics, a stronger hook than a
same-register variant would have given. Chosen over a dielectric-
breakdown/Lichtenberg-figure piece (this gallery's own long-standing
backlog item, researched again this session and again passed over) since
Ising's stochastic Metropolis update was a genuinely new architectural
register — every existing agent/field piece is deterministic given its
seed — where dielectric breakdown would have been closer in kind to
PHYSARUM/SANDPILE's existing deterministic-aggregation registers.

## Piece 9: SWARMALATOR — Oscillators That Sync and Swarm (2026-07-06)
O'Keeffe, Hong & Strogatz, "Oscillators that sync and swarm" (Nature
Communications 8, 1504, 2017): a minimal generalization of the Kuramoto
model in which N point agents each carry BOTH a 2D position and a phase,
and the two are coupled in both directions — phase similarity biases
spatial attraction, and spatial proximity biases phase synchronization.
Five parameter regimes (J, K) produce five qualitatively different
collective states, observed in real sperm, Japanese tree frogs, and
magnetic colloid suspensions.

  dx_i/dt = (1/N) sum_j!=i [ dir_ij(1 + J cos(th_j-th_i)) - dir_ij/r_ij^2 ]
  dth_i/dt = (K/N) sum_j!=i sin(th_j-th_i) / r_ij

### Why this piece is architecturally different
Every earlier piece couples its agents/field INDIRECTLY: PHYSARUM's agents
only sense a shared trail field, CHLADNI's grains only descend a shared
plate-mode gradient, MEMBRANE/LENIA/EXCITABLE diffuse through immediate
neighbors via a Laplacian kernel. SWARMALATOR is the first with DIRECT
all-pairs coupling — every agent's motion depends on every other agent's
exact position and phase, every frame, with no mediating field at all.
Implemented as a single fragment shader where each of AGENTS_DIM^2
invocations loops over the ENTIRE population (a `for` loop bounded by a
JS-templated compile-time constant, ${N_TOTAL}=1296, exactly like CHLADNI/
QUASICRYSTAL bake N_STRIKES into their shader source), sampling every other
texel of the same ping-ponged state texture — an O(N^2) simulation packed
into one pass. VTF-rendered via gl.POINTS exactly like PHYSARUM/CHLADNI's
agents, but with a fundamentally different interaction topology (dense
graph, not field-mediated). AGENTS_DIM=36 (1296 agents, not the ~65k-150k
of the field-mediated pieces) is the direct cost of O(N^2): correct choice
verified to hold ~49fps under software rendering (SwiftShader) at this
count — a much larger N would need either a coarser update rate or a
spatial-hash/tree approximation to stay real-time.

Also the first piece needing NO boundary condition of any kind — unlike
PHYSARUM (toroidal wrap), CHLADNI (reflective plate edge), or GYRUS (hard
confinement circle), the attraction/repulsion balance is self-confining:
the swarm finds its own equilibrium size (empirically ~1.0 world-space
radius for N=1296) from the physics alone, with no explicit geometric
constraint written anywhere in the shader.

### Phase IS hue — the same quantity drives the dynamics and the color
theta maps directly to HSV hue. This isn't a cosmetic choice: it means
synchronization is legible AS color coherence rather than needing a
separate visualization layer — a single-hue disk literally IS the
synchronized state, a smooth rainbow ring literally IS the phase-sorted
state, confetti literally IS the incoherent state. No other piece in the
gallery has this direct an identity between its core physical quantity
and its rendered color.

### Tuning traps hit this session (read before reusing all-pairs coupling)
1. **A dense random seed detonates the repulsion term.** First attempt
   seeded 1296 agents uniformly in a small random disk (r<0.35). The
   1/r_ij^2 repulsion term spikes hard on any near-neighbor pair — with
   1296 points crammed into a small disk, some pairs are always
   pathologically close — launching a handful of agents outward in one
   violent burst that then takes 15-20 real seconds to walk back in (the
   same over-eager-force-integration trap GYRUS hit with unclamped spring
   forces, just via a different force law). Fixed by seeding on a jittered
   GRID at roughly the model's own equilibrium spacing (span=1.7 world
   units over a 36x36 grid, ±0.6-cell jitter) instead of a small random
   disk — this alone cut the settle-to-crystal time from ~20s+ (with a
   large transient overshoot up to r=2.3 along the way) down to a clean,
   monotonic settle finishing by ~t=15s with no overshoot past the final
   radius. General lesson for any 1/r^2-type force: seed near the
   force-balance density, not near the final *shape*.
2. **Full J,K interpolation across mode switches doesn't work here, unlike
   CHLADNI's (m,n).** First attempt continuously interpolated J,K between
   modes (CHLADNI's precedent: no reseed, glide the parameters). This
   fails structurally: STATIC SYNC (K=1) is an ABSORBING state — once
   every phase converges to the exact same value, sin(th_j-th_i)=0 for
   every single pair, permanently, since nothing in the dynamics can ever
   re-introduce phase diversity by just changing K under an already-
   uniform phase field. Every mode after STATIC SYNC inherited a totally
   flat phase field and could never show ANY of its own structure (order
   parameter stayed pinned at 1.00 through STATIC PHASE WAVE too). Fixed
   by reseeding on every mode switch and snapping J,K directly — the same
   "structural regime change, not a continuously-interpolable parameter"
   reasoning SANDPILE already used for its 5 modes; CHLADNI's continuous
   glide only works because its target FIELD changes shape with (m,n),
   whereas an absorbing state in phase space poisons every later mode
   regardless of what the field does next.
3. **The phase-sorted "ring" states need MUCH more than one comfortable
   mode-hold to self-organize from pure random phases.** O'Keeffe et al.'s
   headline analytical result — that STATIC PHASE WAVE spontaneously sorts
   phase by spatial angle (phi ~ theta) even from random initial phases —
   is a real energy-minimization result, but for a finite N=1296 system
   integrated with plain forward Euler, discovering that sort from
   scratch is a huge combinatorial rearrangement that didn't visibly
   complete within a 28-30s test (colors stayed visibly unsorted noise on
   top of a perfectly good crystal lattice). Fixed with a per-mode
   `phaseBias` seed parameter: phase is seeded as the agent's grid angle
   from center plus jitter of amplitude pi*(1-phaseBias) (0 = pure random,
   matching a full circle of noise; 1 = phase locked exactly to angle).
   STATIC SYNC/STATIC ASYNC use phaseBias=0 (they don't need any spatial
   correlation to reach their target state); STATIC PHASE WAVE/SPLINTERED
   use 0.85 and ACTIVE uses 0.7 — enough of a head start that the ~30s
   hold is spent *refining* a rough sort into a clean ring/scramble
   instead of *discovering* the sort from nothing, the same role GYRUS's
   oversized seed loop played for its confinement circle. This is an
   initial-condition choice, not a shortcut around the dynamics — the
   position/phase equations are untouched; only what they start from
   changed.
4. **SPLINTERED PHASE WAVE (K=-0.1) is barely distinguishable from STATIC
   PHASE WAVE (K=0) in a still frame — an honest limitation, not a bug.**
   Swept K in {-0.1,-0.2,-0.3,-0.45} against the same phaseBias=0.85 ring
   seed, watching for the paper's described "fracturing into clusters":
   none of these visibly fractured the ring shape within a 28s hold. A
   direct measurement (RMS positional drift over a 2s window after a 25s
   settle) confirmed the difference is real but is a MOTION difference,
   not a shape difference: K=0 drifts 0.012/2s (essentially frozen), K=-0.1
   drifts 0.028 (~2.3x more, matching the paper's own "quivers with small
   oscillations" description), K=-0.3 drifts 0.035, and K=-0.75 (ACTIVE)
   drifts 0.051 (~4.3x). The mode is scientifically correct as tuned — a
   subtly trembling ring rather than a visually fractured one — but a
   viewer glancing at a screenshot won't see it, only a viewer watching
   the live piece for several seconds will. Noted here honestly rather
   than cranking K up to force a more dramatic still image, which would
   have meant abandoning the paper's actual example value.

### Live order parameter readout
R = |(1/N) sum_i e^(i*theta_i)|, the field's own standard synchronization
metric, computed every 12 frames via gl.readPixels off the (tiny, 36x36)
agent texture and displayed top-right — the same "show the real computed
number, don't hardcode a label" precedent GYRUS set with its live GI
readout. Requires WEBGL_color_buffer_float to read a float framebuffer;
guarded with a try/catch that silently disables the readout (leaving the
"—" placeholder) if unsupported, rather than throwing. Correctly reads
~1.00 for STATIC SYNC, ~0.00-0.05 for every other mode — including STATIC
PHASE WAVE, where R is expected to be near zero even in the "organized"
ring state, since a full rainbow of phases spread evenly around a circle
still sums to zero. R alone can't distinguish STATIC PHASE WAVE (organized
ring) from STATIC ASYNC (disorganized confetti) or ACTIVE (turbulent) —
all three read ~0 — so it's a companion to the visual, not a replacement.

### 5 Modes (34s hold, full reseed + J,K snap on every switch)
- STATIC SYNC:           J= 1.0, K= 1.0   — crystal-like disk, one hue
- STATIC PHASE WAVE:     J= 1.0, K= 0.0   — rainbow ring, phase sorted by angle
- SPLINTERED PHASE WAVE: J= 1.0, K=-0.1   — same ring, quivering (see trap 4)
- ACTIVE PHASE WAVE:     J= 1.0, K=-0.75  — continuously churning confetti
- STATIC ASYNC:          J=-1.0, K=-1.0   — static confetti disk, no rhythm

### Interaction
Click/touch drops a decaying "spark" (6-slot ring buffer of position/time,
the same pure-function-of-elapsed-time pattern as QUASICRYSTAL's ripples/
CHLADNI's strikes/GYRUS's growth stimulus) that both attracts nearby
agents and pulls their phase toward a common value — a localized flash
entraining its neighbors, the way one firefly's flash nudges nearby
fireflies toward its own rhythm. Verified via a headless tap on a 390x844
mobile viewport: order parameter visibly perturbed, nearby agents pulled
toward the touch point.

### Verification
Headless Playwright + SwiftShader confirmed: ~49fps sustained (N=1296,
O(N^2) coupling) with no console/page errors, no shader compile failures
despite the 1296-iteration shader loop (a real risk on some ANGLE/D3D9
backends that force full loop unrolling — untested on that specific
backend, noted as an open risk for future sessions), STATIC SYNC reaching
R=0.99-1.00 by ~25s, STATIC PHASE WAVE producing a genuine smooth rainbow
ring by the same point, STATIC ASYNC/ACTIVE showing confetti-random hues
(R~0), the click/touch spark visibly perturbing nearby agents on both
desktop and a 390x844 mobile portrait viewport with touch events, and the
live R readout tracking all of the above correctly.

### Inspiration
Web research into 2026 generative-art trends, following up on this
gallery's own backlog ("Kuramoto oscillator synchronization... researched,
not used" — flagged across two prior sessions researching SANDPILE and
GYRUS). A search for Kuramoto visualization surfaced "swarmalators" —
O'Keeffe, Hong & Strogatz's 2017 extension coupling the Kuramoto model to
a swarming/flocking model — as a sharper, more novel target than plain
Kuramoto synchronization alone: it's a genuinely two-way coupling (phase
affects position AND position affects phase) rather than agents merely
carrying a phase on top of independent movement, and it unifies this
gallery's two prior registers (PHYSARUM's agent-based swarming, CHLADNI's
oscillator-driven pattern) into one model neither piece individually
covers. Chosen over a plain single-purpose Kuramoto piece specifically
because the position/phase FEEDBACK loop gave it a genuinely new
architectural requirement (direct all-pairs coupling) that no existing
piece in the gallery had attempted.

## Piece 8: SANDPILE — Abelian Sandpile / Self-Organized Criticality (2026-07-05)
The Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld sandpile (Phys. Rev. Lett., 1987): grains fall on
a finite lattice; any site holding 4+ grains topples, giving one grain to
each of its 4 neighbours; a toppled neighbour can itself go unstable, so
a single grain can trigger a cascade of any size. The system tunes ITSELF
to the edge of stability with no external parameter to adjust — the
namesake example of self-organized criticality, now cited across
earthquakes, forest fires, neuronal avalanches, and market crashes as
instances of the same underlying mathematics.

### Why this piece is architecturally different
Every prior piece is a CONTINUOUS field (PDE diffusion, closed-form wave
superposition, or a gradient-descending particle) — this is the first
DISCRETE integer-threshold cellular automaton in the gallery. Same
ping-pong float-texture architecture as MEMBRANE/LENIA/EXCITABLE (R
channel = state, ping-ponged every pass), but the update rule is "if
h>=4, topple" rather than a Laplacian or kernel convolution. Rendered
with NEAREST filtering (no linear smoothing anywhere, unlike every other
piece) so the lattice reads as flat quantized colour cells, matching
Bak's original sandpile figures rather than this gallery's usual smooth
continuous glow.

### The single most important tuning insight: throughput is rate-limited
by the CA rule itself, not by injection amount
First attempt injected a tiny fractional trickle per relaxation pass
(0.01 grains/pass) reasoning naively from "real BTW piles drive
infinitely slowly." After 11 real seconds the pile was a ~15px dot —
at that rate it would take literal hours to fill the frame. The fix
required understanding the actual bottleneck: with "at most one topple
per site per pass" (see below), a single source cell can only ever SHED
exactly 4×dissip grains to its neighbours per relaxation pass, no matter
how much is poured in — so total system throughput is hard-capped at
(grains/pass/source × sources), and the only way to grow a satisfying
fractal pattern within a ~30s mode hold is to push per-pass injection
UP toward that cap (not fractionally below it) while cranking the
number of relaxation passes per frame (RELAX_STEPS=24) so many passes
of real toppling happen between rendered frames. Final tuning:
srcRate=0.93/pass, pourRate=4.0/pass, 24 passes/frame — reaches a richly
detailed, screen-filling fractal pattern in 25-30s. General lesson for
any future threshold/capacity-limited automaton: work out the actual
per-step throughput ceiling the update rule imposes before tuning the
injection rate against a "feels realistic" intuition — the two can be
orders of magnitude apart.

### Capped-topple design (one topple per site per pass, not floor(h/4))
A naive parallel-toppling shader computes `topples = floor(h/4)` and
lets a single pass drain a tall stack completely in one shot, which is
the standard trick for computing a sandpile's FINAL stable state fast.
That trick was tried first and rejected for this piece: it lets one
large injection (e.g. a held click) flood the entire visible pile in a
single rendered frame, since a very tall stack can dump dozens of grains
onto each neighbour at once. Replaced with `topple = h>=4 ? 1 : 0`,
sending exactly one grain per topple regardless of how tall the stack
is — a cell holding 40 grains needs 9 more passes to fully drain. This
makes cascades visibly propagate outward one cell per relaxation pass,
like a wavefront, instead of an instant flood — the correct choice for
a piece meant to be watched, not just solved.

### Activity channel doubles as the "cascade flash" and free noise filter
G channel holds a decaying "just toppled" pulse (`act = act*0.82 +
topple*0.95`, clamped to 3). Decaying PER PASS (not per frame) means
that with 24 passes/frame, any activity from early passes in a frame
has almost fully decayed (0.82^20 ≈ 0.02) by the time the LAST pass's
result is rendered — so the screen only shows a bright flash where
toppling happened in the last few passes before render, letting settled
regions read as flat quantized colour instead of a permanent white
wash. This fell out of the ping-pong architecture for free; no explicit
temporal filtering was written.

### Palette
4-stop gradient keyed to grain count: void (h=0) → slate blue (1) → teal
(2) → gold (3, right at the topple threshold) → hot orange/white (4+,
transient mid-cascade only, since a site never rests above 3). The
"just toppled" activity channel adds a warm white overlay on top,
so actively cascading regions flash bright against the quantized
resting palette — this pairing is what produces the correct
"crystalline fractal disc with a hot cascading core" look matching real
BTW sandpile identity-element images.

### 5 Modes (34s hold; mode switch clears the lattice — a structural
regime change, not a continuously-interpolable parameter like CHLADNI's
(m,n), so reseed-on-switch follows LENIA/PHYSARUM's precedent instead)
Same toppling shader throughout; only drive geometry / dissipation /
boundary differ per mode — same "one engine, different constants"
economy as CHLADNI's mode numbers or GYRUS's repulsion radii.
- CLASSIC:   single point source, dissip=1.0 (conservative), open
  boundary — Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld's original 1987 test case
- RICE PILE: 4 scattered point sources merging into one lattice — after
  Frette et al., Nature 1996, the real long-grain-rice pile experiment
  that demonstrated tunable criticality in an actual granular system
- SEISMIC:   dissip=0.90 (each topple loses 10% of its grains as
  friction) — after Olami, Feder & Christensen 1992's non-conservative
  earthquake-fault variant; visibly smaller/denser than CLASSIC at the
  same real-time budget since dissipated grains never reach the rim
- NEURONAL:  8 randomly-scattered point sources, regenerated fresh each
  time the mode is entered — after Beggs & Plenz 2003's cortical
  neuronal avalanches, where SOC-like statistics emerge from distributed
  spontaneous activity rather than one driven point; produces amoeba-
  like merging blob clusters, visibly distinct from RICE PILE's 4 fixed
  points
- TOROIDAL:  periodic (wraparound) boundary instead of an open sink,
  otherwise identical to CLASSIC — after Dhar 1990's formal result that
  a sink is required for the stable/recurrent configuration set to form
  the abelian group the model is named for; without one (a closed torus)
  the system never truly settles. Visually near-identical to CLASSIC
  until the pattern grows large enough to reach the wraparound edge
  (didn't clearly happen within one 34s hold in testing at this piece's
  fill rate — an honest limitation noted here for future tuning, e.g. a
  faster fill rate specifically for this mode, rather than silently
  claiming a divergence the current build doesn't reliably show)

### Interaction
Press-and-hold (mouse or touch) pours sand at the cursor at the same
per-pass rate as the throughput cap (pourRate=4.0/pass), producing an
immediate, visible local cascade distinct from the ambient drip source
— verified via a held touchstart/touchend pair producing a second,
separate blob away from the auto-driven pile.

### Verification
Headless Playwright + SwiftShader confirmed: no console/page errors,
correct fractal growth over 5s/15s/30s in CLASSIC (matching real BTW
sandpile "identity element" imagery — solid outer ring, intricate
fractal mid-band, hot core), all 5 modes producing visibly distinct
structures (RICE PILE's 4 merging disks, SEISMIC's smaller/denser
disk from dissipation, NEURONAL's amoeba-like random blob clusters,

above), press-and-hold pour producing an independent splash blob on

existing pieces under the same software-rendering harness (9.5 vs.
9-14 for LENIA/CHLADNI/PHYSARUM) after tuning RELAX_STEPS down from an
initial 32 to 24 passes/frame.

### Inspiration
Web research into 2026 generative-art trends surfaced curl-noise flow
fields (already well-trodden by every piece in this gallery in spirit)

and dielectric-breakdown/Lichtenberg-figure models as under-used subjects
in creative coding. Chose the Abelian sandpile over dielectric breakdown

## Piece 7: GYRUS — Cortical Folding via Differential Growth (2026-07-04)
A closed loop of ~100-550 nodes grows inside a fixed circular "skull"
boundary. Immediate loop-neighbors are held apart by a short spring;
ALL nearby nodes (not just loop-neighbors) repel each other across a
slightly larger radius — that mismatch is the whole engine. Local
crowding stretches some neighbor-edges past a threshold, and a
stretched edge splits in two (a cell dividing). More curve keeps
arriving than the boundary has room for, so the loop has nowhere to go
but to fold — the mechanism proposed for real cortical gyrification
(Tallinen, Chung, Biggins & Mahadevan, "On the growth and form of
cortical convolutions," Nature Physics 2016). This is a 2D closed-curve
toy version of that paper's 3D bilayer FEM model, an honest
simplification in the same spirit as MEMBRANE's kernel or CHLADNI's
mode shape.

### Why this piece is architecturally different
The gallery's first piece with no GPU simulation at all: plain
Canvas2D, a few hundred/thousand JS objects, a spatial hash grid for
neighbor queries. Every earlier piece is a WebGL field or VTF-rendered
particle swarm; this is the "single growing self-avoiding polyline"
idea from the long-standing differential-growth backlog entry
(untouched since session 3), finally used, applied to a folding
membrane rather than a coastline/leaf-margin (the usual differential-
growth subject).

### Five modes = real comparative-neuroanatomy GI points
MOUSE (lissencephalic) → CAT → MACAQUE → HUMAN → DOLPHIN (bottlenose,
whose cortex is *more* convoluted than a human's despite lacking a
layered prefrontal cortex — the intended "novel and unexpected" fact
this piece is built around). Modes vary repulsion radius; a smaller
repulsion radius packs more folding into the same skull. GI (contour
length / convex-hull perimeter, the real metric) is computed live from
the running node positions every 8 frames and shown in the UI — not a
hardcoded number. Figures are illustrative, not exact literature values.

### Tuning traps hit this session (read before reusing this technique)
1. **Numerical blowup from plain force*dt integration.** First version
   moved nodes by raw `pos += force*DT` with no velocity damping or
   step clamp — positions diverged to ~1e221 within a few seconds
   (classic explicit-Euler instability once combined spring+repulsion+
   confinement stiffness exceeds the stability bound for the timestep).
   Fixed by giving every node a persistent velocity, damping it by 0.72
   each step, and hard-clamping per-step displacement to half the
   target edge length — same pattern CHLADNI already used for its
   grains (`vel *= uDamping; clamp to uMaxV`), just not carried over.
2. **A filled self-intersecting polygon reads as shattered glass.**
   At the density this force model actually produces, the closed curve
   self-crosses under simple pairwise repulsion (an honest limit of
   this toy model — it doesn't do real segment-segment collision
   avoidance). `ctx.fill(path, 'evenodd')` on a self-intersecting path
   throws that up as ugly overlapping fragments. Switched to rendering
   the tissue as a THICK round-jointed STROKE instead of a filled
   polygon — the same self-crossings just read as tissue folding over
   itself, not a bug. General lesson: a stroke tolerates topological
   mess a fill cannot.
3. **Growth rate must be paced against real time, not population
   percentage.** `quota = population * 2%/frame` hit each mode's node
   cap in ~2 real seconds regardless of cap size, giving no visible
   "unfolding" — it just looked instantly tangled. Fixed with a
   fixed-real-time growth budget (`(maxNodes - seedCount) /
   GROWTH_FRAMES` nodes/frame, accumulated fractionally) so every mode
   takes the same ~25s to reach its cap regardless of magnitude.
4. **Small seed radius never expands to fill its confinement.**
   Starting from a tiny seed circle (SEED_R=0.11 in a CONFINE_R=1.0
   world) produces a small compact tangled clump that never grows
   large enough to reach the boundary — repulsion prevents local
   overlap but supplies no long-range "pressure" to inflate the whole
   mass outward. Fixed by starting the seed loop already spanning most
   of the confinement (SEED_R=0.55) so folding happens as local
   wrinkling along an already-large loop — incidentally closer to real
   fetal cortical development (already sheet-like before it folds) —
   and every mode now visibly fills its skull instead of leaving a lot
   of dead black space.
5. **A soft confinement spring can be overwhelmed by crowding
   pressure**, letting the mass spill past the intended boundary at
   high node counts (observed clearly breaching the skull ring at
   30s in an early build). Added a hard positional clamp (project back
   onto the circle, zero the outward velocity component) as a genuine
   wall on top of the soft spring — the physical skull cannot be
   pushed through.
6. **GI is a nonlinear, oversensitive dial once the hull reaches the
   boundary.** An analytic maxNodes formula (target GI × boundary
   circumference / edge length, assuming efficient packing) was wrong
   by 3-8x in practice — once the loop's convex hull is already at the
   confinement radius, any extra curve length has nowhere to go but
   fold, so small node-count changes cause disproportionate GI swings.
   Final per-mode maxNodes values (115/175/250/380/550) were found by
   directly fast-forwarding the simulation (calling relax()/grow() in
   a tight loop via page.evaluate, no real animation frames needed) and
   reading back the actual settled GI — bisecting empirically was far
   faster and more reliable than re-deriving the formula.

### Interaction
- Click/touch: a localized decaying outward "growth stimulus" (4-slot
  ring buffer of position/time, the same pattern as QUASICRYSTAL's
  ripple buffer and CHLADNI's strike buffer — a pure function of
  elapsed time, no per-stimulus cleanup needed) bulges nearby tissue
  outward for ~2.6s, prompting extra local folding near the touch
  point — thematically a localized proliferative growth spurt, not a
  generic "poke."

### Verification
Headless Playwright + Canvas2D (no WebGL/SwiftShader needed — first
piece that doesn't touch WebGL at all) confirmed: stable node positions
across all 5 modes over a full 42s hold with no numerical blowup, a
strictly monotonic GI ladder across MOUSE→DOLPHIN (~1.7 → ~2.8 in the
final tuning, order preserved run to run despite RNG jitter in node-
insertion offsets), 60fps sustained even at DOLPHIN's ~550-node cap, a
390×844 mobile portrait viewport keeping the skull circle centered and
UI unclipped, and the click/touch growth-stimulus bulge visibly
deforming the ribbon without throwing.

### Inspiration
Web research into 2026 generative-art/creative-coding trends surfaced
differential growth again (Anders Hoff/inconvergent's differential-
line, jasonwebb's 2d-differential-growth-experiments) alongside
Genuary 2026 prompts ("one line," "unexpected path") — both pointing at
a single growing self-avoiding polyline, an idea flagged back in
session 3 as "researched, not used." Reframing it as cortical
gyrification (real Tallinen et al. 2016 mechanism) rather than generic
coral/leaf-margin growth gave it a physically-grounded story and, via
comparative neuroanatomy, a genuinely surprising fact to build the mode
list around (dolphins out-fold humans).

## Piece 6: CHLADNI — Vibrating-Plate Nodal Patterns (2026-07-03)
Sand grains on a square plate migrate off the moving antinodes and collect
on the still nodal lines of the plate's current vibration mode — the
phenomenon Robert Hooke first noticed in 1680 and Ernst Chladni published
figures of in 1787, later named "cymatics" by Hans Jenny (1967). First
audio-reactive piece in the gallery.

### Why this piece is architecturally different
Every prior piece either simulates a continuous field on a ping-pong
framebuffer (MEMBRANE/LENIA/EXCITABLE) or a closed-form field with no
state at all (QUASICRYSTAL), and PHYSARUM's agents deposit into a second
ping-ponged trail field that itself diffuses and decays. CHLADNI has
agents (grains) but NO trail/field buffer whatsoever: ~65k grains
(256×256 float texture of x,y,vx,vy, ping-ponged, VTF-rendered exactly
like PHYSARUM's ) are drawn directly to the visible framebuffer every
frame. The "pattern" isn't stored anywhere — it only exists as the
instantaneous spatial distribution of live particles. This is the
simplest state model yet (one texture, no second buffer) despite having
the most physically-motivated per-particle dynamics so far.

### Field and grain physics
- field(x,y) = cos(nπx)cos(mπy) − cos(mπx)cos(nπy), the standard
  free-square-membrane approximation used throughout digital cymatics
  art (NOT exact clamped-plate Kirchhoff eigenmodes with Bessel
  functions — noted in-code as a knowingly-approximate model, same
  honesty as LENIA's simplified kernel)
- Grains do not sample a precomputed field texture — each grain
  evaluates `field()` itself, 5× per frame (center ± epsilon in x and
  y), to build a central-difference gradient of field², and descends it:
  `vel -= grad(f²) * uForce`. This pulls grains off antinodes (f²
  large) toward nodal lines (f²≈0) exactly like real sand being
  vibrated off the moving parts of the plate — the pattern is *revealed
  through motion*, not painted.
- Ambient per-grain hash-noise jitter simulates the vibration itself
  (without it grains freeze at the first local f²-minimum they hit,
  including false minima off the true nodal set); `uDamping=0.90` bleeds
  excess kinetic energy each frame; velocity is hard-clamped
  (`uMaxV=0.05`) to keep the explicit-Euler integration stable against
  the steep gradient spikes near high-order modes.
- Reflective (not toroidal) boundary at the plate edge — a real plate is
  a bounded object. Grains observably pile up along the rim over time;
  this is an accepted emergent behavior (real shaken sand does collect
  at a container's edge), reinforced rather than fought by rendering the
  plate as a discrete bordered square object (see composition below),
  not fought as a bug.

### Composition: a bounded object, not a full-bleed field
Every earlier piece fills the entire viewport with its field. CHLADNI
instead renders a centered square "plate" (78% of the shorter viewport
dimension) with pure black void outside it — a deliberate compositional
break to match the subject: a Chladni plate is a physical instrument on
a stand, not an infinite medium. Plate background is a near-static
brushed-metal gradient with a slow specular sheen drifting across it and
a bright rim stroke; it is intentionally NOT modulated by the field
value, so the nodal pattern is legible ONLY through grain position, the
way it actually is in a real experiment (the metal itself doesn't glow
along the nodes — only the sand shows you where they are).

### 6 Modes (m, n plate mode-numbers; 30s hold + 4.5s eased glide between)
- FUNDAMENTAL: m=1, n=3  — Ernst Chladni, 1787
- CROSSWEAVE:  m=2, n=5  — nodal line interference
- ROSETTE:     m=3, n=6  — Hans Jenny, cymatics, 1967
- LATTICE:     m=4, n=7  — FEM modal-analysis register
- MANDALA:     m=5, n=8  — acoustic-levitation traps, Marzo 2015
- TEMPEST:     m=6, n=11 — high-order harmonic turbulence
Mode numbers are continuously interpolated (not hard-cut) during the
4.5s transition, so the plate visibly passes through chaotic, scattered,
"off-resonance" states between clean figures — true to how a real plate
looks away from an exact resonant frequency, and a nice bonus: it means
there's no special-case code needed to animate the transition, the same
gradient-descent physics that produces the resting figure also produces
the transient chaos for free.

### Audio (first audio-reactive piece in the gallery)
Two mutually-exclusive opt-in toggle buttons (top right), off by default
so the piece is silent and permission-free until a user explicitly asks:
- TONE: starts a sine oscillator whose frequency glides continuously as
  `55 * sqrt(m² + n²)` — the real proportionality between a membrane's
  resonant frequency and its mode indices — tracking the interpolated
  (m,n) every frame via `setTargetAtTime`. The pitch and the shape change
  together because they're driven by the same numbers; sound literally
  *is* the shape here, not a separate reactive layer bolted on.
- MIC: `getUserMedia` → `AnalyserNode` (fftSize 1024). RMS of the time-
  domain buffer feeds grain-jitter "excitation" energy (louder = more
  scatter); the dominant frequency bin in the 80–2000Hz band is mapped
  log-scale onto the 6-mode list (continuous interpolation between
  neighboring modes' m,n, smoothed 0.04/frame) so the plate reacts to
  whatever sound is actually in the room. Falls back to holding the last
  good (m,n) when the loudest bin is near noise floor, rather than
  snapping to silence-noise. Toggling MIC off resets the auto-cycle
  schedule's clock to "now" so it eases forward from the current state
  instead of jumping to wherever the untouched schedule would have
  reached.
- Verified both toggles run without throwing under Playwright with
  `--use-fake-ui-for-media-stream --use-fake-device-for-media-stream`
  (mic path) and a plain click (tone path); real audible output wasn't
  (and can't be) verified headless, only the absence of exceptions and
  correct downstream state changes (button "on" styling, plate tint
  shift, mode label updates from the fake mic's fake tone).

### Interaction
- Click/touch = strike the plate: pushes a decaying, spatially-localized
  outward impulse into the grain velocities near the click point (4-slot
  ring buffer of strike position/time, same pattern as QUASICRYSTAL's
  ripple buffer — pure function of elapsed time, no per-strike state to
  clean up) and adds a global burst of jitter energy. Grains audibly-
  named "scatter" then resettle back onto the nodal lines over a few
  seconds — verified visually via headless screenshots: a strike
  visibly explodes the FUNDAMENTAL figure into a radial burst pattern
  around the click point, then converges back to the original nodal
  lines within ~6s.

### Verification
Headless Playwright + SwiftShader screenshots confirmed: correct nodal
figures for FUNDAMENTAL and CROSSWEAVE (matching expected mode-number
line counts), clean mode-transition crossfade, strike-then-resettle
behavior, TONE/MIC button state and plate-tint changes, and a mobile
portrait viewport (390×844) keeping the plate square and UI unclipped.

## Piece 5: QUASICRYSTAL — Wave-Interference Aperiodic Lattice (2026-07-02)
Closed-form superposition of N plane waves evenly spaced by angle π/N. When N
is not a multiple of 4 the field is aperiodic — a mathematical quasicrystal:
long-range orientational order without translational periodicity, the
structure Dan Shechtman discovered in an Al-Mn alloy in 1982 (forbidden
fivefold diffraction symmetry, 2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry). Same family of
pattern produced today by laser interference lithography for photonic
bandgap materials.

### Why this piece is architecturally different
Every prior piece (MEMBRANE, LENIA, EXCITABLE, PHYSARUM) is an iterative
PDE/agent simulation requiring a ping-pong framebuffer and per-frame state
history. QUASICRYSTAL has none of that: the field is a pure closed-form
expression (sum of cosines) evaluated fresh per pixel per frame from
`uTime` alone. No simulation texture, no framebuffers, single draw call.
Deliberately chosen as a break from the "biological continuous field" run
of the first four pieces — crystalline/mathematical register instead of
organic — after web research turned up quasicrystal interference lithography
as a good tonal fit for a "novel and unexpected" net.art piece.

### Technical Stack
- Pure WebGL1, no extensions required at all (no OES_texture_float — the
  first piece in the gallery that needs none, since there's no float
  texture state to carry between frames)
- field(p) = (1/N)·Σ cos(p·dir_i·freq + phase_i), dir_i at angle i·π/N + slow
  rotation drift; phase_i uses a golden-angle-ish increment (i*2.399963) so
  the N waves desync from each other over time instead of pulsing in unison
- Chromatic dispersion: R/G/B channels sample the field at freq·(1±disp),
  producing prismatic fringing at bright ridges — literal optical accuracy
  (different "wavelengths" refract at different frequencies), confined to
  peak regions via a smoothstep mask so it doesn't wash out the troughs
- Facet edge lines: thin bright contours at fract(field·lines) ridges,
  masked to peaks only — reads as crystal facets catching light, not a
  topographic contour map (see palette tuning trap below)
- Palette: 3-stop smoothstep gradient (near-black trough → jewel mid-tone →
  bright peak), NOT an IQ hue-rotating cosine palette — see trap below

### Palette tuning trap (future sessions: read this before using IQ cosine
palettes for anything with iso-contour edge lines)
First attempt used the classic IQ cosine palette (a + b·cos(2π(c·t+d))) like
MEMBRANE, driven by the raw field value. Combined with per-ridge contour
lines at multiple fract() levels, this produced a loud rainbow topographic-
map look (blue/green/yellow/orange bands) that didn't match this gallery's
moody, void-dominant aesthetic (compare EXCITABLE's controlled indigo→
purple→cyan→white ramp). Fixed by switching to a 3-stop smoothstep gradient
per mode (near-black → single jewel hue → bright highlight) AND masking the
edge lines / chromatic fringe to only the top ~30% of field intensity via
smoothstep(0.35, 0.9, t), so most of the frame stays black and only ridges
glow. Also had to push the black→jewel transition later (smoothstep(0.15,
0.55,t) → smoothstep(0.38,0.68,t)) — the first version filled too much of
the screen with midtone color instead of staying void-dominant. General
lesson: when a shader value has many iso-contour crossings, a hue-rotating
palette will read as a contour map; use a single-hue-per-mode gradient with
highlights gated to peaks instead, verified via screenshot before shipping.

### 5 Modes (auto-cycle every 32s, named after real quasicrystal symmetries)
- PENTAGONAL:   N=5,  icy sapphire/ice-blue — Shechtman's original 1982 find
- HEPTAGONAL:   N=7,  amber/gold
- OCTAGONAL:    N=8,  emerald/jade
- DECAGONAL:    N=10, rose/magenta — real Al-Ni-Co decagonal quasicrystals
- DODECAGONAL:  N=12, violet with strong prismatic fringing (fringe=0.42,
  vs 0.15-0.20 for the other modes) — the most complex tiling gets the most
  visible chromatic dispersion, tying the effect to the concept

### Interaction
- Mouse/touch: drops a decaying ripple (circular wave, exp decay in both
  age and distance) into the interference field at the click point — reads
  as a lattice defect/excitation propagating outward. Up to 6 concurrent
  ripples in a circular buffer (uRippleP/uRippleT uniform arrays); each
  ripple's contribution is purely a function of elapsed time since it was
  dropped, so no per-frame state/texture needed to age or clear them out.
- Verified visually via headless Playwright screenshots: all 5 modes show
  correct N-fold rotational structure, ripple click carves a visible dark
  cavity with prismatic fringing at nearby ridges.

## Piece 2: LENIA — Continuous Cellular Automata (2026-06-29)
WebGL implementation of Bert Chan's Lenia (2018). A single continuous field [0,1]
evolves under dx/dt = G(K*x), where K is an annular Gaussian kernel and G is a
bell-curve growth function. Produces emergent organism-like patterns.

### Technical Stack
- Pure WebGL1 + OES_texture_float (~230 lines)
- Ping-pong framebuffer, same architecture as MEMBRANE
- 11×11 annular Gaussian kernel (ring peak at normalized radius 0.5, ~85 taps)
- 2 simulation steps per frame, sim resolution capped at 512px
- Bioluminescent palette: void→indigo→cyan→jade→white
- Iridescent hue drift via col.gbr swizzle mix (slow oscillation)
- Radial vignette + bloom on high-state cells

### Organism Modes (auto-cycle every ~40s, reseed on switch)
- bloom:   mu=0.200, sigma=0.040, dt=0.100
- weave:   mu=0.150, sigma=0.022, dt=0.090
- fronds:  mu=0.265, sigma=0.044, dt=0.100
- crystal: mu=0.305, sigma=0.056, dt=0.110
- pulse:   mu=0.183, sigma=0.033, dt=0.090

### Interaction
- Mouse/touch: seeds living material (~0.75) at cursor position
- On mode change: full reseed with random circular blobs

### Kernel detail
K(r) ∝ exp(-((r/5 - 0.5)*4)² * 2)  — peaks at r≈2.5px, zero beyond r=5.5
G(n) = 2*exp(-((n-mu)²)/(2*sigma²)) - 1  maps [0,1] → [-1,1]

## Piece 3: EXCITABLE — FitzHugh-Nagumo Excitable Media (2026-06-30)

- turbulent:  a=0.7, b=0.8, ε=0.16, I=0.90 — chaotic spiral breakup

## Piece 4: PHYSARUM — Slime-Mould Agent Simulation (2026-07-01)
WebGL1 agent-based simulation of Physarum polycephalum transport-network
formation (Jones 2010; Tero et al. 2010 "Rules for Biologically Inspired
Adaptive Network Design" — the Tokyo-rail-network slime-mould study). First
agent-based piece in the gallery — everything before it was a continuous PDE
field (Gray-Scott / Lenia / FitzHugh-Nagumo). Distinct visual register:
thin glowing branching networks on black, vs. the previous pieces' filled
organic fields.

### Technical Stack
- Pure WebGL1 + OES_texture_float, no WebGL2 / transform feedback needed
  (earlier sessions had flagged Physarum as "needs WebGL2" — untrue; a
  gl.POINTS + vertex-texture-fetch (VTF) architecture works fine in WebGL1)
- Agent state (x, y, heading, species) lives in a float texture, ping-ponged
  by a fragment shader exactly like the other pieces' simulation textures
- Deposits are rasterized with gl.POINTS whose VERTEX shader reads each
  agent's position via texture2D (VTF) — no CPU readback, no scatter buffer
- Trail field (two channels, one per species) is a second ping-ponged float
  texture: deposit (additive blend) → diffuse (9-tap box blur) → decay
- AGENTS_DIM=384 (147,456 agents), TRAIL_RES=512
- Two competing species: each senses its own trail channel, is repelled by
  the other's (`sensed = own - repulsion*other`) — produces territorial
  boundaries between colonies, not just single-species webs
- Palette: warm amber/yellow (real Physarum polycephalum's colour) vs.
  crimson/magenta, with a hot glow blended in at contact seams

### Sense/turn/move rule (classic Physarum algorithm)
- Each agent samples trail intensity at 3 points: straight ahead (C), and
  ±sensorAngle to either side (L, R), all at sensorDist from current position
- If C is highest: keep heading. If L or R is highest: turn toward it by
  rotAngle. If tied: turn a random direction (hash of texcoord + frame count)
- Move forward by `step` along the new heading; position wraps via fract()

### 5 Modes (auto-cycle every 35s, full reseed of agents + trail on switch)
- NETWORK:   sensorAngle=25° sensorDist=11px rotAngle=28° repulsion=0.08 — clean transport webs
- TERRITORY: sensorAngle=22° sensorDist=9px  rotAngle=32° repulsion=0.65 — competing colony boundaries
- COLONY:    sensorAngle=45° sensorDist=20px rotAngle=16° repulsion=0.05 — slow wide branching
- SWARM:     sensorAngle=12° sensorDist=7px  rotAngle=50° repulsion=0.00 — turbulent flocking streams
- LICHEN:    sensorAngle=32° sensorDist=14px rotAngle=20° repulsion=0.20 — dense space-filling mycelium

### Deposit tuning (mean-field equilibrium)
Trail intensity converges to T_eq ≈ deposit·(agentsPerSpecies/texelCount) / (1-decay).
With decay in 0.90–0.978 and ~73,728 agents/species over 262,144 texels, deposit
must be tiny (0.014–0.06) or the field saturates solid within ~40 frames — this
was the single biggest tuning trap. Target T_eq around 0.15–0.2 so trail-dense
regions (where agents cluster via the positive feedback loop) stand out brightly
above a dim, mostly-black ambient field. Don't reason about deposit amount in
isolation — always check it against decay and agent density together.

### Interaction
- Mouse/touch: boosts both trail channels at cursor (like a food source),
  drawing nearby agents toward it via the normal sensing rule

### WebGL1 gotchas hit during this build (future sessions: check these first
if a ping-pong sim renders solid black or shows no motion)
1. **REPEAT wrap + non-power-of-two texture = silently broken.** WebGL1
   requires CLAMP_TO_EDGE for any NPOT texture; REPEAT on NPOT makes the
   texture "incomplete" and every sample returns black — with NO GL error.
   TRAIL_RES=640 (chosen to match membrane.html's cap) broke this way, and
   AGENTS_DIM=384 broke it a second time for the *agent* texture. Fix: only
   use REPEAT where toroidal wrap is actually needed (the trail field) and
   keep those textures power-of-two; leave everything else CLAMP_TO_EDGE.
2. **gl.ARRAY_BUFFER is global, shared, mutable state — bind it explicitly
   every time.** A `bindQuad()` helper that calls `vertexAttribPointer`
   without first calling `gl.bindBuffer(ARRAY_BUFFER, quad)` silently
   captures whatever buffer some *other* draw call last bound (here, the
   points buffer from the deposit pass), so the "full-screen quad" render
   degenerates into a handful of stray triangles covering almost none of
   the target texture. This produced a bizarre symptom: the sim texture
   alternated frame-to-frame between "genuinely updating" and "frozen at
   its initial random seed," because the corrupted quad draw was a near
   no-op that left the ping-pong texture untouched on the affected frames.
   Every VS/FS pair that shares an attribute name across programs should
   bind its own buffer inside its bind helper — never rely on ambient state.
3. Headless/software-rendered testing (SwiftShader via Playwright) is slow
   per-primitive but NOT the cause of blank renders — don't assume "it's
   just slow," force a synchronous gl.readPixels() to check real buffer
   contents before chasing a performance theory.

### 2026-07-05 — Session 8
- Added SANDPILE: the Abelian sandpile model / self-organized criticality
  (Bak, Tang & Wiesenfeld, 1987). First DISCRETE cellular automaton in
  the gallery — every prior piece is a continuous field (PDE diffusion,
  closed-form wave superposition, or a gradient-descending particle);
  this is an integer threshold-toppling rule on the same ping-pong
  float-texture architecture MEMBRANE/LENIA/EXCITABLE already use.
- Biggest lesson of the session: the toppling rule itself imposes a hard
  throughput ceiling (a source cell can only ever shed exactly
  4×dissipation grains per relaxation pass, no matter how much is poured
  in), so a naive "drive infinitely slowly" trickle (reasoning from how
  real BTW sandpiles are studied) produced a pile that would have taken
  literal hours to fill the frame. Fixed by pushing per-pass injection
  UP toward the actual throughput cap instead of fractionally below it,
  and cranking relaxation passes per frame (24/frame) so many toppling
  steps happen between rendered frames — reaches a screen-filling
  fractal pattern in 25-30s. Also switched from a `floor(h/4)`-per-pass
  toppling rule (which lets one large injection flood the whole visible
  pile in a single frame) to a capped "one topple per site per pass"
  rule, so cascades visibly propagate outward like a wavefront instead
  of instantly flooding — full write-up under the SANDPILE technical
  notes above, worth reading before building any other threshold/
  capacity-limited automaton.
- 5 modes reuse the identical toppling shader with different drive
  geometry/dissipation/boundary: CLASSIC (Bak-Tang-Wiesenfeld's original
  single-point case), RICE PILE (Frette et al. 1996's real granular
  experiment, 4 merging sources), SEISMIC (Olami-Feder-Christensen 1992's
  lossy earthquake variant), NEURONAL (Beggs & Plenz 2003's cortical
  avalanches, 8 randomly-scattered sources reseeded per mode entry), and
  TOROIDAL (Dhar 1990's no-sink closed-lattice result via a periodic
  boundary) — the last mode didn't visibly diverge from CLASSIC within
  one 34s hold at the tuned fill rate, noted honestly rather than
  claimed as a success.
- Verified via headless Playwright/SwiftShader: no console errors, all
  5 modes visually distinct (except TOROIDAL vs CLASSIC as noted), the
  press-and-hold pour interaction producing an independent splash on
  both mouse and touch, a mobile portrait viewport, and FPS in the same
  ballpark as existing pieces after tuning relaxation-pass count down
  from an over-eager first guess of 32/frame to 24/frame.
- Inspiration: web research into 2026 generative-art trends (curl noise,
  flow fields — territory this gallery has already covered in spirit)
  pointed toward looking further afield; landed on self-organized
  criticality over a dielectric-breakdown/Lichtenberg-figure alternative
